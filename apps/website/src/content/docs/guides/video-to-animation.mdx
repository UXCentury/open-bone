**Animate from video** turns a clip of a person into an animation: OpenBone follows the body through the video and
keys your rig's bones to it. It's a fast way to rough out a walk, a wave or a dance, which you then clean up and
exaggerate by hand.

Tracking runs on your computer with MediaPipe's Pose Landmarker, which ships with the editor. It works offline, and the
video never leaves your machine.

## What makes a good clip

- **One person**, with the **whole body in view** (or at least the upper body for arm motion).
- A steady camera, good light, and clothes that don't hide the joints.
- For a side-view rig, film from the side; for a front-facing rig, film from the front.
- Short is fine: a few seconds for a cycle.

## Animate

<Steps>

1. Open your rig and choose **From video…** in the Timeline toolbar (or ☰ Menu → **Animate from video…**).

2. **Choose video…** to pick an MP4, WebM or MOV file, or **Use webcam** → **● Record** (a 3-second countdown, up to
   30 seconds, **■ Stop** to end early). Set **From** and **to** to use part of the clip.

3. Press **Track body**. The tracked skeleton is drawn over the video: play or scrub the video to check it.

4. Check the **Bones** list. Each bone follows a body part, guessed from its name:
   - *upper arm, forearm, hand, thigh, shin, foot* with a side (`left`/`right`, `l`/`r`), *spine*/*torso*, *neck*,
     *head*, and *hip*/*pelvis* for the body's movement;
   - side-view rigs named `front-`/`back-`: pick which side of the body is the **front**;
   - bones in an **IK chain** are left to the constraint, and its **target** follows the ankle or wrist instead.

   Change any row, or **+ Animate another bone…**. **Mirror** flips the video left to right.

5. Choose where the keys go: a **new animation**, or an existing one at a start time (keys in the clip's span are
   replaced, the rest stay). Then:
   - **Body movement**: *Move with the body*, *In place* (keeps the bounce: good for cycles) or *Hips stay still*.
   - **Bones point**: *Along the body* (bones follow the limbs' directions) or *Relative to the first frame* (for rigs
     whose bones don't point along the limbs: stand in the rig's pose at the start of the clip).
   - **Smoothing** filters jitter; **Key tolerance** sets how far simplified keys may stray (0 keys every frame).

6. Press **Animate**. It's one undo step.

</Steps>

<Aside type="tip">
**Save tracking…** keeps the tracked body as a `.pose.json` file. **Load tracking…** maps it again later, for example
on another rig, without tracking the video again.
</Aside>

## Tidy up

Tracked motion is a starting point. Typical next steps: loop a cycle by matching the last keys to the first, push the
key poses further, ease the timing in the graph editor, and add secondary motion with physics.

## With an AI agent

With the editor open and connected (**⚡ Connect MCP**), an agent can call `animate_from_video {path}`: the editor
tracks the clip and the server keys the rig, returning the mapping it used. `saveTrack` keeps the tracking, and
`animate_from_pose_track` maps a saved track without the editor. See [MCP](/reference/mcp/).
